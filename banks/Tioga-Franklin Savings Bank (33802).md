## Warning signs in the data

Risk-based capital, which weights assets by how risky they are, told the same story, with a Tier 1 ratio of 2.9% against a median of 14.6% and a total ratio of 4.1% against 15.8%.

Credit quality was weak: loans 90 or more days late or no longer earning interest made up 9.2% of loans, against a national median of 0.5%, and nonaccrual assets plus foreclosed property were 6.8% of assets against 0.3%. The Texas ratio was 257.0%, far above the national median of 2.9%. Profitability was deeply negative, with return on equity at -121.2% against a median of 11.6%, and the efficiency ratio (operating costs as a share of revenue) was 188.7% against 62.1%, meaning costs exceeded revenue.

Funding looked steadier: uninsured deposits, those above the government insurance limit, were 6.4% of deposits against a median of 24.8%, and core deposits, a stable funding source, were 83.1% of assets against 75.5%. Interest expense was higher than typical at 3.4% of assets against a median of 1.6%, and brokered deposits, money placed through middlemen, were 1.1% of deposits against 0.0%. Paper losses on bonds available for sale were modest at 1.6% of equity, below the national median of 5.6%, and losses on bonds held to maturity were 0.0%, matching the median.
