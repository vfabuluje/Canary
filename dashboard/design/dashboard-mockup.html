THE BIG TEST IT FAILED

In 2023, three large US banks collapsed within two months. Canary did not flag any of them in time. It learned from the 2008 crisis, when banks failed from bad loans. These banks failed differently: their bond investments lost value when interest rates rose, and nervous customers pulled their money out fast.

Research tool, not financial advice. Built from public FDIC data.

WHY "CANARY"?

THE NAME

Coal miners used to carry canaries underground. A canary reacts to toxic gas before people do, so if the canary got sick, the miners knew to get out. Canary is meant to be that early warning, for banks.

THE STORY BEHIND IT

In 2020 and 2021, Silicon Valley Bank took in billions in deposits from tech startups and put much of it into long-term government bonds while interest rates were low. In 2022 rates rose fast, and those bonds lost value. Most of SVB's deposits were above the $250,000 the government insures, so customers had a reason to run. In March 2023 they pulled about $42 billion in a single day, and regulators closed the bank on March 10. Signature Bank failed two days later; First Republic in May. The warning signs were in SVB's public filings months earlier.

THE GOAL

Could a system built only on public data have raised the alarm in time? Canary reads every US bank's public numbers each quarter and ranks which look most likely to fail within a year. It learned from failures up to 2015, then was tested on 2016–2024 without peeking. AI helpers write plain-English reports, and every number they use is checked against the data.

THE HONEST ANSWER

It caught 21 of the 29 banks that failed in 2016–2024, usually about a year ahead. It did not catch SVB, Signature or First Republic. Showing why is part of the point.

Research tool, not financial advice.
